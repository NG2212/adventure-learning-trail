## Why this adventure

Every week you sit through meetings that end with "who's sending notes?". By the end of this adventure you will have a prompt that soaks up a raw transcript or your scribbled notes and wrings out a summary people actually read.

You will leave with something real: a recap you sent, and a prompt you keep.

## What makes a good recap

A good recap answers three questions fast: what did we decide, who does what, and what is still open. AI is good at sorting; you are good at judging what matters.

| Part | What it holds | Who checks it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decisions | What we agreed, in one line each | You |
| Actions | Owner, task, due date | You and the owner |
| Open questions | What still needs an answer | The group |

## Before you start

- Pick one real meeting from this week. Notes, a transcript, or a voice memo all work.
- Open the AI tool you already use.
- Remove anything confidential you would not paste into an email.

## Shape of the adventure

You will run one prompt, sharpen it once, and send the result. Then you will save the prompt so next week takes seconds.

## Core task

### Step 1: Soak it up

Paste your notes after this prompt.

```
You are my meeting note-taker. Read the notes below and produce:
1. Decisions (one line each)
2. Action items as a table: owner | task | due date
3. Open questions

Keep it under 200 words. Use plain language. If an owner or date is missing, write "?" rather than guessing.

NOTES:
[paste here]
```

### Step 2: Wring it out

Read the draft. Then ask for the version you would actually send.

```
Rewrite this as a short message to the attendees. Start with a one-sentence summary. Keep the action table. Tone: friendly and direct, like a colleague, not a robot.
```

## Layers

- Ask it to flag anything that sounds like a decision but was never confirmed.
- Ask for a version in another language for a colleague abroad.
- Turn the action table into monday.com items.

## Side quest

Run the same notes through a second AI tool and compare. Which one kept the nuance? Write one line about what you noticed.

## Proof of work

Send the recap to the attendees. Save your final prompt somewhere you will find it next week. That is your badge-worthy proof.
